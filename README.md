# How Quants Think

*How Quants Think* is an educational series dedicated to developing the reasoning and problem-solving mindset behind quantitative finance.

Quantitative finance is not just about mathematical formulas, sophisticated models, or numerical algorithms. It is about understanding which questions to ask, recognizing hidden assumptions, evaluating trade-offs, and knowing when a seemingly reasonable solution might fail in practice.

The series focuses on **how experienced quants approach problems, make modeling decisions, and turn practical challenges into general principles.**

## The Approach

Each episode follows a simple structure:

1. **The Problem** — A realistic question from quantitative finance.
2. **The Obvious Answer** — The intuitive solution that first comes to mind.
3. **The Practical Challenge** — Why that answer may be incomplete or misleading.
4. **The Quant Insight** — The assumptions, limitations, and trade-offs that matter.
5. **The General Principle** — A lesson that can be applied to other quantitative problems.

## Topics Covered

The series is organized around five themes:

- **Thinking Like a Quant** — Understanding models, assumptions, simplicity, and model reliability.
- **Choosing Models and Methods** — Making informed decisions between analytical and numerical approaches.
- **Numerical Thinking** — Understanding accuracy, convergence, stability, and computational limitations.
- **Market Thinking** — Exploring calibration, hedging, market uncertainty, and model risk.
- **Professional Thinking** — Connecting quantitative theory with validation, research, implementation, and real-world practice.

## The Main Idea

The goal is not to memorize more formulas or collect modeling techniques, but to develop a systematic way of thinking about quantitative problems.

A good quant does not simply ask whether a model is correct, but whether it is appropriate for its intended purpose, what assumptions it relies on, and what consequences arise when those assumptions fail.

**In quantitative finance, the first answer is rarely the full answer.**
